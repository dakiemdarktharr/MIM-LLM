\begin{abstract}
This planned study asks whether temporal scope and repeated state changes expose
different failures in short story continuation. We specify premise pairs that change
one event time while preserving key-possession history, and allow several coherent
continuations. An executable state model supplies necessary propositions; human
readers assess their expression, contradictions and writing quality. The study will
contrast required changes with retained invariants, include events written by the
model itself, and test transfer to licensed natural narratives.

\section{Research question and intended contribution}
The question is whether models preserve the temporal scope of conditional rules
while updating overwritten state and separating actual from hypothetical worlds.
The proposed contribution is a controlled measurement method and an empirical
account of this interaction. Releasing a cabinet dataset alone is insufficient.

\textbf{Primary contrast:} the paired change in required action at a fixed decision
time, accompanied by retention of possession and transfer history. \textbf{Planned
hypothesis:} greater overwrite depth will increase state errors, and success on
one-name entity probes will not imply consistent multi-sentence continuation.
A null interaction or a reversed relationship is a publishable observation only
if the test is reliable and the analysis gives independent insight.

Counterfactual generation and causal invariance already appear in
TIME TRAVEL~\cite{timetravel}; Counterfactual Invariance Prediction explicitly
classifies retained events~\cite{cip}. PASTA studies participant-state
perturbation and story revision~\cite{pasta}. Entity tracking
probes~\cite{entity,naturaltracking} and OpenMEVA~\cite{openmeva} motivate
evaluating state, discourse and writing separately. ConStory-Bench already
examines consistency with self-generated narrative history~\cite{constory};
ChronoScope studies temporal scope stability across dialogue turns~\cite{chronoscope}.
Neither invariance, self-history consistency, nor a general gap between single
answers and sequential coherence is a new claim of this project.

The proposed contribution must instead demonstrate additional diagnostic value
for open continuation under controlled temporal rules and state updates.
Effective and no-op interventions inherit behavioral testing principles from
CheckList~\cite{checklist}. FactTrack's time-aware facts~\cite{facttrack} and
ConWriter's external state/repair controller~\cite{conwriter} must be distinguished
from a backbone-only inference condition. ScratchWorld~\cite{scratchworld},
currently a preprint, also motivates separating changed-field performance from
dominant unchanged facts.
\pending{Demonstrate the incremental diagnostic value; the 25 November source
audit narrows the claim but does not establish that the proposed study is novel.}

\section{Task, semantics, and design}
Let \(s^w_{t,k}\) denote state in world \(w\), at time \(t\), with micro-event index
\(k\). It records possession, power, lock status and object locations. A successful
transfer updates possession. A conditional is evaluated at its stated time.
For the controlled task, agents obey the rule, all prefix transfers precede the
decision, the cabinet remains locked immediately before that decision, and no
alternative opening action occurs. An obligation alone would not entail execution
without the compliance assumption. Exclude simultaneous power-return/decision
events initially, or explicitly declare their order.

\begin{quote}\small
Illustration, not model output: Lan passes the key to Huy; Huy passes it to Mai.
At exactly 9:00 the holder must open the cabinet if power has returned.
All agents obey the rule, which is evaluated once at 9:00.
Change only \texttt{8:55} to \texttt{9:05} for the power return.
Mai remains holder in both premises; opening at 9:00 changes to waiting.
If a generated continuation puts the key in a drawer, later text must respect
that choice. A different coherent location is equally acceptable.
\end{quote}

Late power entails waiting and a locked cabinet \emph{at 9:00}; it does not
uniquely entail opening at 9:05 without an additional persistent-trigger rule.
Possession before an action is distinct from possession after a freely generated
storage event. For a 9:05 prefix, ``ten minutes later'' refers to 9:15.

Cross overwrite depth, distractor distance, temporal relation and conditional scope.
Separate direct consequences, invariant facts and optional narrative choices.
The current cabinet generator covers depth 2--5 and one time rule.
Additional rule forms and multiple objects are \textbf{planned extensions}, not
implemented benchmark coverage. Counterfactual text must be attributed to the
hypothetical world before contradiction scoring.
The current pilot uses only 8:55/9:05 and distinct recipients, leaving a
last-recipient plus clock-threshold shortcut. It is a smoke benchmark, not yet
evidence of structural generalization. Add no-op interventions, held-out template
families and shortcut anchors before freezing a research test.

\section{Experiment design}
Start with LSTM, a causal Transformer, MIM-LM and its learned-pair control,
with three seeds each. The planning cap is ten million training targets per run:
\(4\times3\times10^7=120\) million targets before selection runs.
This is an initial evidence budget, not a fluency guarantee.
Use TinyStories~\cite{tiny} for general language training and a source-disjoint
continuation training set if instruction learning is needed. Declare the mixture
and hold it constant. The synthetic smoke corpus is not adequate pretraining.
One-name probes are auxiliary diagnostics.
Consider a separately reported open pretrained model as a capability reference
to detect a writing/instruction floor; it is not a resource-matched architecture
control. Report semantic-only success and format compliance beside the primary
strict metric. Self-event assessment with visible full history establishes
behavioral consistency, not the storage mechanism of hidden recurrent memory.

\textbf{Critical linguistic analysis:} classify scope mistakes, stale possession,
world mixing, invalid temporal ordering and inconsistent self-written locations.
Compare effect versus depth at matched length, and effect versus length at fixed
depth, to distinguish overwritten state from generic context difficulty.
